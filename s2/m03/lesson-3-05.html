Un dîner au restaurant

Reading & Culture · about 50 minutes
Le tableau du module : Paul Cézanne, Pommes et oranges (Still Life with Apples and Oranges), c. 1899

A short evening at a restaurant, told course by course. It uses your vocabulary, your prendre, and your partitives — all at once. Read and listen, then step back to see why UNESCO decided the French meal itself is a treasure worth protecting. Before you read: a few words you haven't formally studied, glossed here so the passage reads smoothly:
  • ce soir — tonight
  • nous dînons — we're having dinner
  • apporte — brings
  • le poisson — the fish

Ce soir, nous dînons au restaurant. Le serveur apporte la carte et nous regardons le menu.

Comme entrée, je prends une salade. Comme plat principal, je prends du poisson avec des légumes.

Comme dessert, je voudrais une tranche de gâteau. À la fin, le serveur apporte l'addition. Tout est délicieux !

IIANNOTE LE TEXTE8 min

The passage is below. In two steps, circle every partitive (du, des), then underline the one quantity expression. Tap the words: the page marks them for you. Notice how de la would appear before a feminine food — and ask yourself why gâteau takes de, not du, here.

IIILE REPAS GASTRONOMIQUE DES FRANÇAIS12 min

In 2010, UNESCO added le repas gastronomique des Français — the French gastronomic meal — to its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Not a single dish, but the whole ritual: a structured meal in a fixed order — l'entrée, le plat principal, le fromage, le dessert — shared slowly with family and friends. The dinner you just read follows that same order (it simply skips the cheese course).

The point of the honor was conviviality: the meal as a moment to be together, to talk, to take your time. In 2022, UNESCO added a second French entry — the craftsmanship of the baguette, and the daily culture around it. When you order in order and linger over l'addition, you are taking part in something a global body decided is worth protecting.

IVUNE VERTU À TABLE5 min
LA COURTOISIE · COURTESY

Notice how the diner speaks: je voudrais, s'il vous plaît — never a blunt demand. French table culture treats the meal as a shared act, and the language of ordering is built on courtesy. To be served well, you greet, you soften your requests, you thank.

Courtesy isn't decoration — it's how a shared table stays pleasant for everyone at it. The small politeness of s'il vous plaît is the whole convivial meal in miniature. « La politesse est à l'esprit ce que la grâce est au visage. » (Politeness is to the mind what grace is to the face.)

Write a five-line restaurant order
