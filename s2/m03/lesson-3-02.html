Lesson 3.02
⚜ Prendre & le partitif — Ordering & “Some”
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you learned the parts of a meal. Today you learn the one verb that lets you order them — and how to say “some.”
First, ten more words — quantities and the table. Then the workhorse verb of the French table: prendre (to have / to take). At a restaurant you don't “eat” a dish, you prends it. We'll also review the partitive (du, de la, de l', des) — how French says “some” cheese, “some” salad.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and pronounce the quantity and table words (words 11–20).
2
I will be able to conjugate prendre and choose the right partitive article (du / de la / de l' / des).
📚 PART 1 · LES QUANTITÉS & LA TABLE 13 min

Ten more words — listen, then repeat

These carry how much and the table itself. Notice the quantity words all end in de — une tranche de, un verre de. Hold onto that; it becomes tomorrow's grammar. Click the 🔊 and say each one back.

  • prendreto take / to have (food)
  • une tranche dea slice of
  • un morceau dea piece of
  • une bouteille dea bottle of
  • un verre dea glass of
  • assez deenough of
  • la spécialitéthe specialty
  • l’assiettethe plate

  • avoir faimto be hungry

🧠 PART 2 · LE VERBE PRENDRE 14 min

The verb you order with

Prendre means “to take,” but at the table it means “to have” a dish or drink: Je prends une salade. It's irregular — the singular forms are easy, but watch the double n in ils prennent.

SubjectForm of prendreExample
jeprendsje prends une entrée
tuprendstu prends un dessert
il / elle / onprendelle prend de la soupe
nousprenonsnous prenons le menu
vousprenezvous prenez une boisson
ils / ellesprennentils prennent le plat du jour

Hear three of them in real restaurant sentences:

  • Je prends une entrée et un plat principal.I'll have a starter and a main course.
  • Elle prend de la soupe.She's having some soup.
  • Nous prenons le menu à vingt euros.We'll take the twenty-euro set menu.
✅ PART 3 · LE PARTITIF — SAYING “SOME” 13 min

When you have some of a food (not all of it, not a specific one), French uses the partitive. It matches the gender and number of the noun:

ArticleUsed before…Example
dumasculine noundu fromage — some cheese
de lafeminine nounde la salade — some salad
de l'before a vowelde l'eau — some water
desplural noundes légumes — some vegetables
  • Je voudrais du fromage et de la salade.I would like some cheese and some salad.

Choose the partitive that fits. Instant feedback — this is practice, not a grade.

✎ ITEM 1
Elle prend ______ soupe.  (la soupe is feminine)
✎ ITEM 2
Je voudrais ______ fromage.  (le fromage is masculine)
✎ ITEM 3
Nous prenons ______ légumes.  (les légumes is plural)
