⚜ Les instruments & la règle n°1 — Indirect Object Pronouns
Lesson · about 50 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you named the party. Today you finish the vocabulary — and learn how to say to whom you give and speak.
You'll pick up the instruments and the comparison words, then meet Rule 1 of this module: the indirect object pronouns lui (to him / to her) and leur (to them). They replace à + a person — so instead of je parle à Marc, you say je lui parle. The pronoun goes before the verb. You'll close with a quick recall check on the module's words and your first try at typing them, accents and articles included.

🧠 PART 2 · LA GRAMMAIRE — LUI & LEUR 17 min

Saying “to him,” “to her,” “to them”

When a verb points at a person with à — parler à quelqu'un, offrir un cadeau à quelqu'un, téléphoner à quelqu'un (to phone someone) — you can replace that whole phrase with one word. That word is an indirect object pronoun, and it comes before the verb.

ReplacesPronounExample
à + one person (m. or f.)luije parle à Marie → je lui parle
à + more than one personleurje parle à mes amis → je leur parle
a thing + a person (both pronouns)le / la / les first, then lui / leurLe cadeau ? Je le lui offre.

One more thing about offrir (to give, to offer): it ends in -ir but takes -er endings.

j'offretu offresil / elle offre
nous offronsvous offrezils / elles offrent

Notice that lui covers both “to him” and “to her” — French doesn't change it for gender here. And in the passé composé, the pronoun still goes first, right before the helping verb: je lui ai téléphoné.

Hear the three examples. Listen for where the pronoun sits — always just before the verb:

  • Je lui offre un cadeau.I give her a gift.
  • Je leur parle.I talk to them.
  • Je lui ai téléphoné hier.I phoned him yesterday. (passé composé)
