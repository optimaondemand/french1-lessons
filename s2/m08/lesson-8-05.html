IIILA FÊTE DE LA MUSIQUE10 min

Every 21 June since 1982, France holds la Fête de la Musique — a free, nationwide celebration where anyone can play music in the streets, on squares, and in parks. Amateur and professional, classical and rap, a lone guitarist and a full band all share the same night. The idea was simple: music belongs to everyone. It has since spread to more than 120 countries.

Music also fills le 14 juillet (Bastille Day) with open-air concerts and the bal des pompiers — the firefighters' dance. And no voice defines French song like Édith Piaf: her La Vie en rose is still, decades later, the sound the world hears when it imagines France.

Why La Vie en rose mattered. Piaf wrote the words in 1945, just after Paris was freed at the end of the Second World War, and first sang it on stage in 1946. For four years Paris had lived under German occupation, with little food, little freedom and much fear. La Vie en rose (life in pink: life seen through rose-coloured glasses) is a simple song about the joy of being in love, and to people who had lived through the war it sounded like hope coming back. The record came out in 1947 and travelled the world: it sold a million copies in the United States, and in 1998 it entered the Grammy Hall of Fame.

IVUNE VERTU FRANÇAISE5 min
LE SERVICE · SERVICE

Édith Piaf (1915–1963) grew up poor and began by singing in the streets of Paris. When she became a star, she used her success to help young singers find their own. In 1944 she sang with the young Yves Montand at the Moulin Rouge, in Montmartre, and helped him with his songs; a few years later he was one of the most famous singers in France. In the early 1950s she helped launch Charles Aznavour, taking him on tour with her and singing his songs. Both became stars because she shared her stage with them.

Service means using what you have been given to lift others up. A celebration is never only about ourselves: when you invite someone, sing for them or offer them a gift, you are practising the same generosity.
