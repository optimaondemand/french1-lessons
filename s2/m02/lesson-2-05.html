Un tour des régions

Reading & Culture · about 50 minutes
Le tableau du module : Claude Monet, Promenade sur la falaise, Pourville (The Cliff Walk at Pourville), 1882
TODAY'S FOCUS
See every word and rule from Week 1 living inside one short tour of France — and hear it read aloud.
A traveler crosses four famous regions in three short paragraphs, using your vocabulary, your demonstratives, and your prepositions all at once. Read and listen, then step back to see why France feels like many countries in one — and why its people hold so tightly to where they're from. You'll finish by writing your own five-sentence description of a region — your rehearsal for tomorrow's Langue en action and the Speaking Task.
ILA LECTURE15 min

Read each paragraph, then press ▶ Écouter and follow along with your eyes. Read it a second time with the audio playing — that pairing of eye and ear is where fluency starts.

A few words you haven't formally studied, glossed here so the passage reads smoothly:
  • beaucoup de — a lot of
  • différentes — different
  • cette année — this year
  • je visite — I visit / I'm visiting
  • il fait chaud — it's hot (weather)
  • historique — historic
  • j’adore — I love

La France a beaucoup de régions différentes. Cette année, je visite quatre régions célèbres.

En Bretagne, à l’ouest, il y a la mer et de belles côtes. En Alsace, à l’est, les villages sont très jolis.

En Provence, dans le sud, il fait chaud et ensoleillé. La Normandie, au nord, a une côte historique. J’adore ces régions !

IIANNOTE LE TEXTE8 min

On a printout or in your notes, circle every preposition of place (en, à l’, dans le, au) and underline every demonstrative (cette, ces). Then match each region to its compass direction — west, east, south, north. Four regions, four corners of France.

IIIL'IDENTITÉ RÉGIONALE12 min

France is unified — one language, one flag — but it is deeply regional, and Paris is only one part of the story. Each region guards its own character:

  • La Bretagne has Celtic roots and its own regional language, le breton, still taught and spoken today — closer to Welsh than to French.
  • La Normandie is home to Mont-Saint-Michel, the island abbey rising from the tidal flats of the north coast.
  • L’Alsace, on the German border in the east, shows German-influenced architecture and cuisine — timber-framed houses, choucroute (sauerkraut), and a dialect all its own.
  • La Provence, in the sunny south, is known for its lavender fields and a Mediterranean climate — chaud et ensoleillé.

This is the module's essential question in one sentence: the land shapes the people. A coast makes fishers and sailors; a border makes a blended culture; a warm south makes a slower, sun-lit way of life.

IVUNE VERTU FRANÇAISE5 min
LA RESPONSABILITÉ · RESPONSIBILITY

When a regional language like le breton nearly disappeared in the twentieth century, it was ordinary people — teachers, families, musicians — who took responsibility for keeping it alive: bilingual schools (Diwan), festivals, and songs passed down at home. Nothing forced them to; they simply refused to let their heritage vanish.

Belonging to a place is not only a feeling — it is a duty of care. What we inherit, we are responsible for handing on.

VDÉCRIS UNE RÉGION15 min

Write five sentences about a region you'd visit

Pick a region and describe it. Use at least five different place-words, one demonstrative (cette région, ces plages), one preposition of place (en, dans le sud de), and one adjective from Module 1. This is your rehearsal for both the Speaking Task and Langue en action — write it well and reuse it.

Checklist before you stop:  □ a demonstrative that matches its noun  □ en / au for “to” a place  □ a compass locating phrase  □ a landscape word  □ an adjective describing the region.
