Une journée en ville

Reading & Culture · about 50 minutes
Le tableau du module : Gustave Caillebotte, Rue de Paris, temps de pluie (Paris Street; Rainy Day), 1877
TODAY'S FOCUS
See a whole day in the city told in the past — and hear it read aloud.
Julie narrates a Saturday in town with her sister. Every verb of coming and going is an être verb, and because Julie is a woman, watch the agreement endings pile up: allée, parties, montées, descendues… Read and listen, then step back to see how one man rebuilt the most famous city in the world. You'll finish by writing your own trip through the city — your rehearsal for tomorrow's Langue en action and the Speaking Task — and a quick vocabulary recall before tomorrow's Vocabulary Check.
ILA LECTURE15 min

Read each paragraph, then press ▶ Écouter and follow along with your eyes. Read it a second time with the audio playing — that pairing of eye and ear is where fluency starts.

A few words glossed before you read, so the passage reads smoothly:
  • samedi dernier — last Saturday
  • ma sœur — my sister
  • après — after / afterward
  • près de — near
  • mes amies — my (female) friends
  • ensemble — together
  • très tard — very late
  • Quelle belle journée ! — What a lovely day!

Samedi dernier, je suis allée en ville avec ma sœur. Nous sommes parties de la maison à dix heures.

Nous sommes montées dans le bus au centre-ville. Après, nous sommes descendues près de la grande place.

L’après-midi, mes amies sont arrivées et nous sommes sorties ensemble. Je suis rentrée à la maison très tard. Quelle belle journée !

IIANNOTE LE TEXTE8 min

The passage is below. Work in two separate steps. Step 1: circle only the forms of être, all of them, through the whole passage. Step 2: when every one is circled, go back to the start and underline the participle that follows each one. Tap the words: the page marks them for you. You should find seven. Ask yourself why every ending has an extra -e or -es — who is telling this story?

IIISPOTLIGHT CULTUREL — PARIS, LA VILLE RECONSTRUITE12 min

The Paris of wide boulevards, uniform cream-stone façades, and grand tree-lined avenues is not medieval — it was deliberately built in the 1850s–70s. Under Napoleon III, Baron Georges-Eugène Haussmann tore through the old crowded city and laid out the grands boulevards, parks, and sewers that define Paris today. It was one of the largest urban-renewal projects in history.

Notice how the city is built for movement — exactly the vocabulary of this module. And in 1900, the Métro opened, adding an underground network beneath the boulevards. This is why French city life leans so heavily on public transit: le bus, le tramway, le train, le métro. When Julie takes the bus into the centre-ville, she's living inside Haussmann's design.

LA RESPONSABILITÉ · RESPONSIBILITY

A great city belongs to everyone who moves through it. Public transit works only when each rider does their small part — buying a ticket, giving up a seat, keeping the shared space clean. A boulevard is a lesson in living well together.
