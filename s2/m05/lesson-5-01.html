⚜ En ville — Getting Around the City
Lesson · about 50 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How do you move through a city — and how do you say it in French?
This module answers one big question: how do we narrate a journey through a city in the past? Before we can tell that story, we need the places and the transport. Today you meet the first ten words — the city itself, the ways you get around, and the first verbs of coming and going. You will hear each one spoken before you try it, because a word you can hear is a word you can use. To finish, you'll open the Carte de France and start placing France's major cities on the map.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and pronounce ten French words for the city and getting around it.
2
I will be able to match each spoken word to its meaning by ear.
3
I will be able to find France's regions and major cities (Paris, Lyon, Marseille, Bordeaux…) on the interactive map.

⏰ PART 1 · SE METTRE EN ROUTE 12 min

First, a quick look back

In Module 4 you learned to talk about the past with avoir: j'ai mangé, j'ai vu, j'ai fait. This module meets the other auxiliary — être — used for verbs of coming and going. That's exactly the story a city gives you: arriving, leaving, going up into a bus, coming back home. First, though, we need the map: the places and the transport.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
How do you usually get around your town?
📚 PART 2 · LE VOCABULAIRE DE LA VILLE 18 min

Listen, then repeat

Click the 🔊 on each word and say it back out loud. The first seven name the city and how you cross it; the last three are your first movement verbs — the ones that will take être in the past. Just get them in your ear today.

  • la villethe city
  • le centre-villedowntown / city center
  • le busthe bus
  • le tramwaythe tram
  • le trainthe train
  • la voiturethe car
  • à piedon foot
  • arriverto arrive
  • partirto leave
  • sortirto go out

One phrase to go with la ville: en ville (into town / in town).

Getting around: en bus, en tramway, en train, en voiture (by bus, tram, train, car) — but à pied (on foot).

⭐ POUR ALLER PLUS LOIN · optional · +7 min
Go down the list a second time, but this time shadow each word: play it, pause, and repeat it out loud three times before moving on. Notice how arriver, partir, sortir all describe motion — keep that in mind; it's the secret to Day 2.
📝 PART 3 · RÉVISE TON VOCABULAIRE 10 min

Drill the words with the Vocabulaire page

You don’t have to build your word bank from scratch. Open the Module 5 Vocabulaire study page (in this module) — every word, its meaning, and its audio in one place, as flip cards you can drill by ear. Because your Vocabulary Check is listening-based, practice by hearing the word and recalling its meaning.

Making your own paper or digital cards on top of that is a great memory booster — but it’s optional, not required.
