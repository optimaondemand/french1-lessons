L'accord du participe passé

Agreement with the Subject · about 50 minutes
Le tableau du module : Gustave Caillebotte, Rue de Paris, temps de pluie (Paris Street; Rainy Day), 1877
TODAY'S FOCUS
One rule sets être verbs apart from avoir verbs — and today you master it, then conjugate one all the way through, on your own.
With avoir, the past participle never changed. With être, it does: it agrees with the subject in gender and number, just like an adjective. A woman writes je suis allée; a group writes nous sommes allés. You already know how adjective agreement works — this is the same four endings. You'll close with two short practice rounds: choosing the helper and the ending, then building whole sentences of your own.

ILA RÈGLE DE L'ACCORD12 min

The participle behaves like an adjective

The rule. When the helper is être, the past participle agrees with the subject: add -e if the subject is feminine, -s if plural, -es if feminine plural. (With avoir, it never agrees with the subject — that's the key difference.)

Listen to the same verb, said by a man and then by a woman. In speech the endings allé and allée sound the same — the change is in the writing:

  • Je suis allé au centre-ville.masculine → allé
  • Je suis allée au musée.feminine → allée
Même son · Same sound, four spellings. allé, allée, allés and allées all sound exactly the same: the -e and the -s are silent, so say them all the same way. You can’t hear the ending, so listen for who (il, elle, ils, elles), then write the ending that matches.
