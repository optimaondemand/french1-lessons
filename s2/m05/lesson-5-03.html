Lesson 5.03
⚜ L'accord du participe passé — Agreement with the Subject
Lesson · about 50 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
One rule sets être verbs apart from avoir verbs — and today you master it, then conjugate one all the way through, on your own.
With avoir, the past participle never changed. With être, it does: it agrees with the subject in gender and number, just like an adjective. A woman writes je suis allée; a group writes nous sommes allés. You already know how adjective agreement works — this is the same four endings. You'll close with two short practice rounds: choosing the helper and the ending, then building whole sentences in your head.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to add the correct agreement ending (-é / -ée / -és / -ées) to an être past participle.
2
I will be able to conjugate an être verb through all six persons with correct agreement, without help.
3
I will be able to choose the right helper and agreement ending in a sentence and build short past-tense sentences of my own.
🎵 PART 1 · LA RÈGLE DE L'ACCORD 12 min

The participle behaves like an adjective

The rule. When the helper is être, the past participle agrees with the subject: add -e if the subject is feminine, -s if plural, -es if feminine plural. (With avoir, it never agrees with the subject — that's the key difference.)

Listen to the same verb, said by a man and then by a woman. In speech the endings allé and allée sound the same — the change is in the writing:

  • Je suis allé au centre-ville.masculine → allé
  • Je suis allée au musée.feminine → allée
🔄 PART 2 · LES QUATRE TERMINAISONS 10 min

One participle, four possible endings

SubjectEndingExample (aller)
masculine singular-éil est allé
feminine singular-éeelle est allée
masculine / mixed plural-ésils sont allés
feminine plural-éeselles sont allées

Hear a feminine singular and a plural to fix the pattern in your ear (again — you hear it, but you write the ending):

  • Elle est partie à huit heures.feminine → partie
  • Nous sommes sortis en ville.plural → sortis

✍️ PART 4 · À TON TOUR 8 min

Now conjugate partir (to leave) in the passé composé, with agreement, through all six persons — on paper or in your head. Commit to an answer first, then reveal to check. Trying before you see the answer is what makes it stick.

🧠 CONJUGATE · partir (passé composé)
Write all six forms of partir with être + agreement, then reveal.
je suis parti(e)nous sommes parti(e)s
tu es parti(e)vous êtes parti(e)(s)
il est parti / elle est partieils sont partis / elles sont parties
Same four endings as aller — only the participle stem changed (allé → parti). If you matched all six, you own the agreement rule.

PRACTICE · UNGRADED
BUILD IT YOURSELF 5 min
Build each one in your head — or out loud — before you reveal the answer.
⚡ DÉFI 1
Say it in French: I (f.) went into town with my sister.
Je suis allée en ville avec ma sœur.
aller takes être, and the participle agrees: allée.
⚡ DÉFI 2
Say it in French: We got on the bus downtown.
Nous sommes montés dans le bus au centre-ville.
monter is an être verb.
⚡ DÉFI 3
Make it feminine plural: Ils sont sortis ensemble.
Elles sont sorties ensemble.
être verbs agree with the subject: sorties.
