TODAY'S FOCUS
What you wear says something about who you are — so how do you say it in French?
Welcome to the module the French take more seriously than anyone: la mode. This week you'll shop, describe, and compare clothes — and learn a grammar trick that stops you repeating the same noun over and over. Today you meet ten pieces of clothing. You will hear each one spoken before you ever try to say it. To finish, you'll open the Carte de France and start learning the regions of France.

First, a quick look back

You already know French nouns come with a gender: le for masculine, la for feminine, les for plural. Clothing is no different. As each word appears, notice the little article in front — it's part of the word, and later this week it becomes the key to a grammar shortcut.

IIIEntraînement · Articles and gender, not graded7 min
Rappel · Le genre des noms (clues to the gender, from Lesson 1.02)

You met these clues in Lesson 1.02 (le drapeau, la culture, le monument…). Here they are again, with words from this semester, Module 7 included:

Usually masculine: le
-eau: le manteau, le chapeau, le tableau, le gâteau
-ment: le vêtement, le moment
-age: le fromage (but watch out: la plage !)
Usually feminine: la
-ure: la chaussure, la peinture, la sculpture, la voiture
-tion / -sion: l’exposition, la télévision

People: the word follows the person: l’acteur (a man), l’actrice (a woman). No clue? Many words give none (la robe, le pull, la couleur), so always learn the noun with its article.
