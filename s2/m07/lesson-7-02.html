⚜ Le shopping & le pronom COD — Say It Without Repeating
Lesson · about 50 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you named the clothes. Today you go shopping — and learn the word that replaces the noun so you don't say it twice.
First, ten shopping words: buying, trying on, sizes, prices. Then the module's big idea — the direct object pronoun (le, la, l', les). Instead of “I like the sweater, I wear the sweater,” French says “I like the sweater, I wear it” — je le porte. Rule 1: the pronoun goes before the verb. You'll close with a quick recall check on all twenty words and your first try at typing them, accents and articles included.

Words to know: four colors for talking about la couleur — rouge (red) · bleu / bleue (blue, m. / f.) · noir / noire (black, m. / f.) · blanc / blanche (white, m. / f.). Like most adjectives, a color goes after the noun and agrees with it: le pull rouge, la robe bleue, la robe rouge (rouge already ends in -e, so it does not change).

🧠 PART 2 · LA GRAMMAIRE — LE PRONOM COD 17 min

A word that replaces the thing

A direct object pronoun (in French, le pronom COD) stands in for a noun you've already named, so you don't repeat it. English does this too: “I see the dress — I love it.” The French pronoun you choose must match the gender and number of the noun it replaces, and — here is Rule 1 — it goes before the verb, not after. In a negative, the pronoun stays glued to the verb, inside ne…pas: Je ne l'achète pas.

Replaces…PronounExample
masculine nounleLe pull ? Je le porte.
feminine nounlaLa robe ? Je la trouve jolie.
before a vowel (m. or f.)l'Le manteau ? Je l'essaie.
plural nounlesLes chaussures ? Je les achète.
in a negative (ne…pas)ne + pronoun + verb + pasLe manteau ? Je ne l'achète pas.

Spelling note: acheter and essayer change their stem in every present-tense form except nous and vous. acheter → j'achète, il achète, but nous achetons. essayer → j'essaie, il essaie, but nous essayons. The pronoun does not change this: je l'achète, je ne l'achète pas.

Hear all four. Notice the pronoun always lands right before the verb:

  • J'aime ce pull. Je le porte souvent.I like this sweater. I wear it often.
  • Cette robe ? Je la trouve jolie.This dress? I find it pretty.
  • Ce manteau, je l'essaie.This coat, I'm trying it on.
  • Ces chaussures ? Je les achète.These shoes? I'm buying them.
