Le passé composé

avoir + Regular -er Verbs · about 55 minutes
Le tableau du module : Georges Seurat, Un dimanche après-midi à l’île de la Grande Jatte (A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte), 1884–86
TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you got the words. Today you build the machine that puts them in the past — the passé composé.
First, ten more leisure words. Then the big idea: to talk about the past, French uses two pieces — the verb avoir (to have) plus a past participle. For -er verbs, the participle is easy: swap -er for -é. manger → mangé, regarder → regardé. You'll finish with a short ear-quiz — hear a word, choose its meaning — the exact skill your Vocabulary Check rewards.

IILA GRAMMAIRE — LE PASSÉ COMPOSÉ17 min

Two pieces: avoir + a past participle

The formula. passé composé = avoir (conjugated in the present) + a past participle. For a regular -er verb, the participle drops -er and adds -é: regarder → regardé, jouer → joué, manger → mangé.

First, remember the present of avoir — it is the engine of the whole tense:

Subjectavoir+ participleMeaning
j'aij'ai regardéI watched
tuastu as regardéyou watched
il / elle / onail a regardéhe watched
nousavonsnous avons regardéwe watched
vousavezvous avez regardéyou watched
ils / ellesontils ont regardéthey watched

Hear three real past-tense sentences. Notice: the -é ending sounds exactly like the -er you already know — the difference you hear is the little ai / as / a in front.

  • J'ai mangé au restaurant hier soir.I ate at the restaurant last night.
  • Tu as regardé le match ?Did you watch the game?
  • Il a joué au football samedi.He played soccer on Saturday.
