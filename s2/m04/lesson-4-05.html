Le week-end de Paul

Reading & Culture · about 50 minutes
Le tableau du module : Georges Seurat, Un dimanche après-midi à l’île de la Grande Jatte (A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte), 1884–86
TODAY'S FOCUS
See every past-tense form from Week 1 living inside one real story — and hear it read aloud.
Paul tells you about his weekend — entirely in the passé composé. His story uses your vocabulary, your participles, and even a negation. Read and listen, then step back to a French summer institution: le Tour de France. You'll finish by writing your own last weekend in the past — your rehearsal for tomorrow's Langue en action and the Speaking Task.
ILA LECTURE15 min

Read each paragraph, then press ▶ Écouter and follow along with your eyes. Read it a second time with the audio playing — that pairing of eye and ear is where fluency starts.

Before you read: a few words you haven't formally studied, glossed here so the passage reads smoothly:
  • j'ai passé beaucoup de temps — I spent a lot of time
  • au parc — at the park
  • le soir — in the evening
  • mes cousins — my cousins
  • grande — big
  • Quel week-end ! — What a weekend!

Le week-end dernier, j’ai passé beaucoup de temps avec mes amis. Samedi, nous avons joué au basket au parc.

Le soir, j’ai regardé un film et j’ai écouté de la musique. Je n’ai pas fait mes devoirs !

Dimanche, mes cousins ont rencontré mes amis et nous avons mangé une grande pizza. Quel week-end amusant !

IIANNOTE LE TEXTE8 min

The passage is below. Work in two separate steps. Step 1: circle only the forms of avoir (ai, avons, ont), all of them, through the whole passage. Step 2: when every one is circled, go back to the start and underline the participle that follows each one. Tap the words: the page marks them for you. You should find six or more. Find the negative sentence — je n'ai pas fait — and notice how ne…pas wraps only avoir.

IIILE TOUR DE FRANCE12 min

Every July, France stops to watch le Tour de France — the annual multi-stage cycling race first run in 1903. Over about three weeks, riders cross the country, climbing the Alps and the Pyrenees before the finish on the Champs-Élysées in Paris.

The overall leader wears le maillot jaune — the yellow jersey — and defends it stage after stage. It is a national summer institution: whole towns line the roads, and millions watch on TV. When a French family talks about what they did last weekend in July, chances are « on a regardé le Tour » is part of it.

IVUNE VERTU FRANÇAISE5 min
LA PERSÉVÉRANCE · PERSEVERANCE

A Tour de France rider covers roughly 3,500 kilometers in three weeks — through heat, rain, and mountain passes that break most people who merely watch. No single day wins the race; the yellow jersey is won by showing up and pushing again, every single stage.

That is what the past tense is really for: it lets you look back and see the effort you put in, day by day. Small repeated acts — a little practice, a little courage — are what add up to something you're proud to have done.

⭐ POUR ALLER PLUS LOIN · optional · +7 min · comprehension check

VTON PROPRE WEEK-END15 min

Write five sentences about your real last weekend

Tell the story of le week-end dernier in the passé composé. Use at least five different verbs, one negative (je n'ai pas…), one irregular participle (fait, vu, pris), and one time expression. This is your rehearsal for both the Speaking Task and Langue en action — write it well and reuse it. Write one sentence in each box; the coach ticks each rule as you write and counts your different verbs.
