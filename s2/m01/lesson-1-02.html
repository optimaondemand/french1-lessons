IILA GRAMMAIRE — TROIS ADJECTIFS IRRÉGULIERS22 min

Three forms, not two

Most French adjectives have two forms — masculine and feminine (grand / grande). But beau, nouveau, and vieux have a third: a special masculine form used before a vowel sound, so the words glide together. Learn all three columns.

Hear the feminine and the before-a-vowel forms in real sentences. Notice how nouvel glides straight into appartement:

Three rules for the whole semester

Beyond the three irregulars, keep these boxes handy — every module from now on uses them.

Regular adjectives — the agreement rule. An adjective matches its noun. Feminine: add -e (grand → grande, intelligent → intelligente); -eux → -euse (sérieux → sérieuse, généreux → généreuse, curieux → curieuse, paresseux → paresseuse); -eur → -euse (travailleur → travailleuse); -il → -ille (gentil → gentille). An adjective that already ends in -e stays the same (timide, drôle, jeune). Plural: nouns and adjectives add -s (mes cousines curieuses, mes parents gentils); -eau → -eaux (beaux, nouveaux); an adjective that already ends in -x stays the same (sérieux, vieux).
C’est = it’s / this is. Use it to point to one person or thing: C’est une vieille maison. (It’s an old house.)
Possessives — my, your, his / her. The possessive agrees with the noun, not the owner: mon before a masculine noun, ma before a feminine noun, mes before a plural. Before a vowel sound use mon even for a feminine noun: mon amie. son / sa / ses mean his or her: sa sœur = his sister or her sister; ses amis = his / her friends.
