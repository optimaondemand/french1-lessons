About This Course

Welcome to French 1

French 1 is a full-year course that takes you from your first words in French to the point where you can hold basic conversations, describe your world, and engage with French culture on its own terms. Semester 1 — Découverte (Discovery) — introduces you to the language through the lens of everyday life in France: greetings, school, family, food, and the city of Paris.

This is an asynchronous course, meaning you work through each module on your own schedule within a two-week window. Modules follow the school calendar — due dates are tied to the academic year, not left open-ended. Each module combines listening, reading, speaking, writing, and cultural study, because language is learned by doing all five.

A note on learning French: French is not memorized — it is practiced. The vocabulary checks in this course have one attempt by design. Students who review their module words throughout the two weeks do well; students who try to memorize the night before do not. Treat every module as a two-week immersion, not a two-day sprint.
Communicative Goals

What You Will Learn in Semester 1

By the end of Semester 1 you will be able to:

  • Introduce yourself, greet others, and exchange basic personal information
  • Describe your classroom, school, and daily environment
  • Talk about your family, ages, and personal characteristics
  • Discuss your school schedule and express likes and dislikes
  • Describe your daily routine from morning to evening
  • Talk about leisure activities and what you plan to do
  • Order food and drink and discuss French café culture
  • Ask and answer questions about Paris and French landmarks
  • Use the irregular verbs aller, vouloir, and pouvoir in context
  • Read short authentic French texts with the help of glosses

Grammar You Will Study

Verb system: être (all persons) · regular -er verbs · avoir (all persons) · regular -ir verbs · reflexive verbs in present tense · futur proche (aller + infinitif) · irregular verbs: aller, vouloir, pouvoir

Nouns and adjectives: definite and indefinite articles · adjective agreement (gender and number) · possessive adjectives (mon/ma/mes, ton/ta/tes…)

Sentences and questions: ne…pas negation · partitive articles (du/de la/de l'/des) · question formation with est-ce que, inversion, and question words (qui, où, quand, comment, pourquoi, combien)

Numbers: 1–30 · telling time · days of the week · months of the year

Semester Project

S1 Semester Project — Mon café parisien (VR)

Mon café parisien — Build It, Then Present It

At the end of Semester 1 you will build your own Parisian café in virtual reality and present it in French. You work through this on your own, in your own time — there is no scheduled session to attend and no one you have to meet. The project is worth 100 points and has three parts.

Part 1 — Preparation (20 pts)
  • Complete a short cultural briefing on French café etiquette
  • Plan your café: what it looks like, what it serves, what makes it French
  • Draft the French menu you will build, using S1 food and drink vocabulary
Part 2 — Build and Present (60 pts)
  • Build your café in the VR space: tables, counter, and a menu board
  • Write your menu in French using partitive articles (du café, de la limonade, des croissants)
  • Record a spoken tour of your café — welcome your visitor using the vous register, present the menu, and recommend one item with je voudrais or vous pouvez prendre
  • Point out one detail of French café culture you built in on purpose, and say why
  • Graded on: communicative accuracy, vocabulary use, social register, pronunciation
Part 3 — Written Reflection (20 pts)
  • Write 40–60 words in French describing your café
  • Use present tense verbs from S1 (être, avoir, -er, -ir verbs, reflexives)
  • Structured prompts provided — not a free composition
Final Exam
Semester 1 Final Exam (end of Quarter 2) The S1 Final Exam covers all grammar, vocabulary, and cultural content from M0 through M8. It includes listening comprehension, reading comprehension, structured grammar questions, and short written production. There is no Quarter 1 exam — the final is the only cumulative exam for Semester 1. It counts for 10% of your Semester 1 grade.

Policies

Course Policies

  • Late work: Assignments submitted after the module close date may receive a maximum of 70% of available points. Contact your facilitator before the deadline if you anticipate a conflict.
  • Vocabulary Check attempts: The Vocabulary Check has one attempt only. There are no exceptions. Review your module words throughout the two-week window — not at the last minute.
  • Grammar Quiz attempts: The Grammar Quiz allows two attempts. Your highest score is recorded.
  • VR work: The short VR activities inside modules are formative and ungraded — participation is expected, and a reflection or survey may accompany one as a completion requirement. The end-of-semester VR project is different: it is graded, and it is worth 10% of your semester grade (see Semester Project above). All VR work in this course is done solo, on your own schedule.
  • Accommodations: Students with documented accommodations should contact their facilitator at the start of the semester. Extended time and alternative format arrangements are honored in accordance with Optima's accommodation policy.
  • Academic honesty: All submitted work must be your own. Translation tools may be used as a reference for checking individual words — not for composing or correcting your responses. Work submitted through AI composition tools will not receive credit.
  • Course audio: Every French word, example sentence, and reading passage in this course has a recording. Click the 🔊 speaker button on any lesson page to hear it, and use the Vocabulaire flip-card page to drill your module words by ear. Listening questions on the Grammar Quiz and Vocabulary Check play the same recordings. Headphones are strongly recommended.
