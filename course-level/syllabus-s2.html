About Semester 2

La France en profondeur — France from Within

Semester 2 takes you deeper into the language and deeper into France. You already know how to describe people, places, and routines in the present. This semester you gain the most important new tool in French: the ability to talk about the past. You will also explore France beyond Paris — its regions, its cuisine, its art, its music, and the question of what it means to be French today.

The grammar in Semester 2 is more complex than Semester 1. The passé composé (past tense) and imparfait (descriptive past) each take a full module to introduce and several more to consolidate. Give yourself time. Students who stay current with each module's vocabulary and grammar do significantly better on the gate check and final than those who fall behind.

Building on Semester 1: Semester 2 assumes full control of all S1 grammar. Être, avoir, regular -er and -ir verbs, reflexives, futur proche, and question formation should feel automatic before you begin. If they do not, review the S1 capstone (M8) before starting S2 M1.
Communicative Goals

What You Will Learn in Semester 2

By the end of Semester 2 you will be able to:

  • Describe people's personalities using irregular and position-sensitive adjectives
  • Talk about the regions of France and compare regional characteristics
  • Order a full French meal and navigate restaurant culture
  • Tell what you did in the past using passé composé with avoir
  • Say where you went and how you got there using passé composé with être
  • Describe scenes, settings, and habits using imparfait
  • Refer to people and things without repeating them using object pronouns
  • Discuss French art, cinema, music, and celebrations
  • Compare and rank people and things using comparatives and superlatives
  • Express what it means to be French and connect your own cultural identity

Grammar You Will Study

Adjectives: irregular adjective forms (beau/belle, nouveau/nouvelle, vieux/vieille) · BANGS position rule · demonstrative adjectives (ce/cet/cette/ces)

Past tenses: passé composé with avoir (completed actions) · passé composé with être (movement/state verbs, DR MRS VANDERTRAMP) · imparfait (description, background, habitual past) · distinguishing passé composé and imparfait in context

Pronouns: direct object pronouns (le/la/l'/les) · indirect object pronouns (lui/leur)

Comparison: comparatives (plus… que, moins… que, aussi… que) · superlatives (le/la/les plus…, le/la/les moins…)

Irregular verbs introduced: faire · prendre · venir · voir

Expressions: voudrais for polite wishes and requests · restaurant ordering sequence
Deferred to French 2: Regular -re verbs · subjunctive mood · conditional tense · si-clause conditionals. These are not tested in French 1 and will not appear on any assessment this year.

Semester Project

S2 Semester Project — Ma soirée française (VR)

Ma soirée française — Set the Table, Then Tell the Story

At the end of Semester 2 you will host your own French soirée in virtual reality — set the table, then narrate the evening in French using the past tenses you learned this semester. You work through this on your own, in your own time; there is no scheduled session to attend and no one you have to meet. The project is worth 100 points and has three parts.

Part 1 — Preparation (20 pts)
  • Complete a cultural briefing on French dinner party etiquette (greetings, gift-giving, meal structure, toasting)
  • Plan your menu and your table, course by course, in French
  • Review key vocabulary for food, preferences, and social formulas
Part 2 — Build and Narrate (60 pts)
  • Set your table in the VR space: place settings, courses, and the details that make it a French meal
  • Record a spoken walk-through of your soirée: welcome your guest, name each course, and explain the order the meal follows
  • Narrate how you got ready — past tense required: passé composé for what you did (j'ai préparé, je suis allé(e) au marché), imparfait for description and background (il faisait beau, la table était prête)
  • Point out one French dining custom you built in on purpose, and say why it matters
  • Graded on: communicative accuracy, vocabulary range, past tense use, social register, pronunciation
Part 3 — Written Reflection (20 pts)
  • Write 60–80 words in French describing the soirée after it ended
  • Past tense required: use passé composé for completed actions and imparfait for descriptions and background
  • Structured prompts provided
Write about what you built, not what you read: Your reflection should describe the soirée you set up and narrated — not a retelling of a reading passage or culture page from an earlier module. Two students should not be able to hand in the same reflection.
Final Exam
Semester 2 Final Exam (end of Quarter 4) The S2 Final Exam is cumulative — it covers all French 1 grammar and vocabulary from both semesters, with emphasis on Semester 2 content (past tenses, object pronouns, comparatives). It includes listening comprehension, reading comprehension, grammar identification, and short structured writing in French. There is no Quarter 3 exam. The Final counts for 10% of your Semester 2 grade.
Rank System

Your Rank — La progression

Your semester average determines your rank. The same ranks apply in both semesters. By the end of French 1, the goal is to finish as a Guide or Ambassadeur/Ambassadrice — representing the language and culture with confidence.

Policies

Course Policies

  • Late work: Assignments submitted after the module close date may receive a maximum of 70% of available points. Contact your facilitator before the deadline if you anticipate a conflict.
  • Vocabulary Check attempts: The Vocabulary Check has one attempt only, in both semesters. The S2 vocabulary load is higher than S1 — review module words from day one of each module.
  • Grammar Quiz attempts: The Grammar Quiz allows two attempts. Your highest score is recorded.
  • Gate check — M6: Students who do not reach 80% on the M6 gate check will receive a targeted review plan before advancing to M7. This is not a course failure — it is a structured support mechanism. Contact your facilitator immediately if you receive a review plan.
  • VR work: The short VR activities inside modules are formative and ungraded — participation is expected, and a reflection or survey may accompany one as a completion requirement. The end-of-semester VR project is different: it is graded, and it is worth 10% of your semester grade (see Semester Project above). All VR work in this course is done solo, on your own schedule.
  • Accommodations: Students with documented accommodations should contact their facilitator at the start of the semester. Extended time and alternative format arrangements are honored in accordance with Optima's accommodation policy.
  • Academic honesty: All submitted work must be your own. Translation tools may be used as a reference for checking individual words — not for composing or correcting your responses. Work submitted through AI composition tools will not receive credit.
  • Course audio: Every French word, example sentence, and reading passage in this course has a recording. Click the 🔊 speaker button on any lesson page to hear it, and use the Vocabulaire flip-card page to drill your module words by ear. Listening questions on the Grammar Quiz and Vocabulary Check play the same recordings. Headphones are strongly recommended.
