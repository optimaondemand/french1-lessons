📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Sharpen two very French sounds, then speak — a day in Paris, out loud, on the record.
First a quick clinic on the two sounds that most mark a French accent: the French r (in Paris, la rue, la gare) and the nasal vowels (in jardin, monument, français). Then you'll record your Speaking Task — the day in Paris you've been building all week, using the structures of the whole semester.
🎤 PART 1 · PHONÉTIQUE 13 min

The French r and the nasal vowels

The French r is made in the back of the throat, not the tip of the tongue — a soft, gargled sound. Don't roll it and don't say the English “r.” Listen for it inside these city words:
  • Parisr in the middle
  • la ruer at the start
  • la garer at the end
Nasal vowels send the air through the nose; there's no hard “n” at the end. jardin ends in the in nasal; monument has the on nasal; français has the an nasal. Listen:
  • le jardinnasal “in”
  • le monumentnasal “on”
  • françaisnasal “an”

🎤 Speaking Task · Une journée à Paris
15 pts · 1 submission
SUBMITTED IN CANVAS · MEDIA RECORDING 15 min
  • Record 6–8 sentences describing a day you would spend in Paris, in order.
  • Use at least five Module 8 words and at least three different S1 structures (e.g. aller + infinitive, a partitive, a reflexive, a time, or a question).
  • Speak from notes, not a script read word-for-word — natural delivery counts.
  • This is your Day 4 / Day 6 writing, spoken aloud. You've already built it.
