Lesson 2.03
⚜ Les règles — Elision, Negation & the Full Table
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Two small rules make your -er verbs sound French — and then you'll conjugate one all the way through, on your own.
Rule 1 was matching the ending. Today: what happens when the verb starts with a vowel (j'étudie), and how to say you don't do something (je n'aime pas). Then you'll build the full table — and try one yourself before you see the answer.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use elision before a vowel (je → j') and place the negation correctly (ne…pas around the verb, with n' before a vowel).
2
I will be able to conjugate a regular -er verb through all six persons without help.
🎵 PART 1 · RÈGLE N°2 — L'ÉLISION 10 min

When the verb starts with a vowel

Rule 2. Before a vowel sound, je drops its e and adds an apostrophe: j'. French avoids two vowel sounds bumping together.

je aime  ❌  →  j'aime  ✅. The same glide happens with j'étudie, j'écoute, and j'adore. You will hear this gliding clearly in the negation clip in Part 2 (je n'aime pas).

The endings themselves don't change — only the little word je shrinks to j'. Everything you learned yesterday still holds.

🚫 PART 2 · RÈGLE N°3 — LA NÉGATION 10 min

Saying what you don't do

Rule 3. ne and pas wrap around the verb: je ne parle pas. And when the verb starts with a vowel, ne also elides to n': je n'aime pas.

Listen — you can hear both the elision (n'aime) and the negation sandwich around the verb:

  • Je n’aime pas les devoirs.I don’t like homework.
⭐ POUR ALLER PLUS LOIN · optional · +7 min
Write two negative sentences of each kind — one with a consonant verb (je ne parle pas espagnol) and one with a vowel verb (je n'écoute pas la radio) — and notice that the only difference is whether ne shrinks to n'.
📋 PART 3 · LE TABLEAU COMPLET — PARLER 12 min

All six persons, one verb

This is the model for every regular -er verb in the module. Read each row aloud; play the three you can hear. Remember: parle, parles, and parlent all sound the same — the difference is only on paper.

PersonFormMeaning
jeje parleI speak
tutu parlesyou speak
il / elle / onil parlehe / she / one speaks
nousnous parlonswe speak
vousvous parlezyou (formal/plural) speak
ils / ellesils parlentthey speak
  • Je parle français.je
  • Nous parlons en classe.nous
  • Vous parlez espagnol ?vous
✍️ PART 4 · À TON TOUR 8 min

Now conjugate étudier (to study) through all six persons — on paper or in your head. Watch the je form: étudier starts with a vowel, so it elides. Commit to an answer first, then reveal to check. Trying before you see the answer is what makes it stick.

🧠 CONJUGATE · étudier
Write all six forms of étudier, then reveal.

Same endings as parler — only the stem changed, and je became j' before the vowel. If you matched all six endings, you've got Rule 1 cold.
