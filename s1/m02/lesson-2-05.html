Lesson 2.05
⚜ Une journée à l'école — Reading & Culture
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
See every word and verb from this week living inside one real school day — and hear it read aloud.
A French student tells you about school. The passage uses your vocabulary, your -er verbs, and your negation — all at once. Read and listen, then step back to see how the French school system is shaped, and meet the oldest idea in French literature: la courtoisie.
📖 PART 1 · LA LECTURE 15 min

Read each paragraph, then press ▶ Écouter and follow along with your eyes. Read it a second time with the audio playing — that pairing of eye and ear is where fluency starts.

J’aime beaucoup l’école. J’étudie les maths, le français et l’histoire.

Le matin, j’écoute le professeur et je travaille avec mes amis. Nous parlons français en classe.

Je n’aime pas les devoirs, mais j’adore la musique et le sport. Et toi, tu aimes l’école ?

🖌️ PART 2 · ANNOTE LE TEXTE 8 min

On a printout or in your notes, circle every -er verb and underline its ending. You should find six or more. Find the one negative sentence and notice how n'…pas wraps the verb.

A few words you haven't formally studied, glossed here so the passage reads smoothly:
  • beaucoup — a lot / very much
  • le matin — in the morning
  • avec — with
  • mes amis — my friends
  • mais — but
  • j'adore — I love / adore
  • Et toi ? — And you?
🇫🇷 PART 3 · LE SYSTÈME SCOLAIRE FRANÇAIS 12 min

In France, after primary school students go to the collège (roughly ages 11–15), then the lycée (about 15–18). The school day runs long — often 8h to 17h — but it's broken by a real sit-down lunch, frequently a hot meal in the cantine. Learning alongside others, all day, is the heart of French school life.

At the end of the lycée comes the famous baccalauréat — le bac — the exit exam that opens the door to university. Along the way, students study a full range of matières (subjects): les maths, le français, l'histoire, les sciences, l'anglais, l'art, la musique, le sport — the very words you learned this week.

⚜ PART 4 · UNE VERTU FRANÇAISE 5 min
LA COURTOISIE · COURTESY

The oldest great work of French literature, La Chanson de Roland (around 1100), tells of Roland, a knight of Charlemagne. From these medieval tales of knights grew the code of courtoisie — courtesy: treating others with respect, patience, and honor, even in the middle of a struggle. The word became so central to French life that it still names good manners today.

A classroom is a shared space — you learn alongside others. Courtesy is what makes that possible: listening when the professor or a classmate speaks, waiting your turn, and helping a neighbor who is stuck. The knight's honor and the student's courtesy are the same virtue, centuries apart.
