Lesson 2.02
⚜ Les matières & les verbes en -er — Subjects + Regular Verbs
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you named the classroom. Today you finish your subjects — and learn the verb pattern that lets you say what you actually do there.
You'll pick up the last school subjects and six everyday verbs, then meet the most useful pattern in all of French: the regular -er verb. Nearly all French verbs follow it, so learning it once unlocks hundreds. Rule 1 is simply matching the right ending to the subject.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and pronounce five more school subjects and six regular -er verbs (words 12–22).
2
I will be able to explain how a regular -er verb is built and match its ending to each subject.

🧠 PART 2 · LA GRAMMAIRE — QU'EST-CE QU'UN VERBE EN -ER ? 17 min

One pattern, hundreds of verbs

Most French verbs end in -er (parler, écouter, aimer). To use one, you drop the -er to get the stem (parl-), then add an ending that matches the subject. That's Rule 1 — and once you know these six endings, you can conjugate almost any French verb.

SubjectEndingExample (parler)
je-eje parle
tu-estu parles
il / elle / on-eil parle
nous-onsnous parlons
vous-ezvous parlez
ils / elles-entils parlent

Hear the three you'll use most. Listen closely: parle, parles, and parlent all sound identical — the endings are silent. Only the spelling changes:

  • Je parle français.I speak French.
  • Tu parles anglais ?Do you speak English?
  • Il parle avec le professeur.He speaks with the teacher.
