Lesson 6.05
⚜ Le week-end de Paul — Reading & Culture
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
See everything from this week living inside one real French weekend — and hear it read aloud.
Paul is a lycéen with a free weekend. His plans use your activity words, your aller forms, and your weather chunks — all at once. Read and listen, then step back to see how the French think about leisure, and meet a French writer who spent his own free time learning to govern himself. Keep the week's question in mind: what does what we choose to do with our free time say about us?
📖 PART 1 · LA LECTURE 15 min

Read each paragraph, then press ▶ Écouter and follow along with your eyes. Read it a second time with the audio playing — that pairing of eye and ear is where fluency starts.

Le week-end, j’ai beaucoup de temps libre. Le samedi, je vais souvent à la piscine avec mes amis.

S’il fait beau, nous allons faire du vélo au parc. S’il pleut, je vais au cinéma ou je lis.

Le dimanche soir, je vais faire mes devoirs. Et toi, qu’est-ce que tu vas faire ce week-end ?

🖌️ PART 2 · ANNOTE LE TEXTE 8 min

On a printout or in your notes, circle every form of aller and underline the weather chunks. You should find five or more forms of aller. Notice which ones are “going somewhere” (je vais à la piscine) and which are the near future (je vais faire mes devoirs).

A few words you haven't formally studied, glossed here so the passage reads smoothly:
  • beaucoup de — a lot of
  • avec — with
  • mes amis — my friends
  • ou — or
  • mes devoirs — my homework
  • qu'est-ce que — what
  • ce week-end — this weekend
🇫🇷 PART 3 · LES LOISIRS EN FRANCE 12 min

The French take leisure seriously — it's part of the culture, not an afterthought. Cycling is a national passion: every July the Tour de France, the world's most famous bike race, crosses the whole country over three weeks, so Paul's faire du vélo au parc sits inside a much bigger tradition.

The cinéma is French too, at its root: in 1895 the Lumière brothers in Lyon held the first public film screening in history. France still treats film as an art form, with cinemas in nearly every town.

And then there's the café — where people sit for hours over one coffee, talking, reading, watching the street. The French famously protect their leisure and time off: long lunches, five weeks of paid vacation, quiet Sundays. Free time isn't “wasted” time here — it's where a life happens.

⚜ PART 4 · UNE VERTU FRANÇAISE 5 min
L'AUTONOMIE · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Michel de Montaigne (1533–1592) retired at 38 to a tower on his estate and spent his free time doing something radical: thinking honestly on paper. The result was the Essais — he invented the essay as a form — where he examined his own habits, tastes, and choices without pretending to be certain. His motto was a question: « Que sais-je ? » — “What do I know?”

Montaigne shows that free time is where you govern yourself — nobody assigns it, so what you choose to do with it is entirely you. That's the week's question turned into a practice: choose your leisure on purpose, and choose well. « La plus grande chose du monde, c'est de savoir être à soi. »
