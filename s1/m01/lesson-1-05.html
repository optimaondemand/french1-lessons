Lesson 1.05
⚜ Je m'appelle Lucas — Reading & Culture
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
See every word from this week living inside one real introduction — and hear it read aloud.
Lucas is a lycéen in Lyon. His introduction uses your greetings, your verb être, and your courtesy words — all at once. Read and listen, then step back to see how French speakers actually greet each other, and meet a young woman whose courage changed her country.
📖 PART 1 · LA LECTURE 15 min

Read each paragraph, then press ▶ Écouter and follow along with your eyes. Read it a second time with the audio playing — that pairing of eye and ear is where fluency starts.

Bonjour ! Je m’appelle Lucas. Je suis étudiant à Lyon et j’ai quinze ans.

Comment allez-vous ? Moi, ça va très bien, merci. Voici mon amie : elle s’appelle Camille et elle est française aussi.

Nous sommes contents d’être ici. Au revoir, et à bientôt !

🖌️ PART 2 · ANNOTE LE TEXTE 8 min

On a printout or in your notes, circle every form of être and underline every greeting or courtesy word. Notice that Lucas uses the formal comment allez-vous ? — ask yourself who he might be speaking to.

A few words you haven’t formally studied, glossed here so the passage reads smoothly:
  • à — in / to (a city)
  • j'ai quinze ans — I’m fifteen (years old)
  • voici — here is / this is
  • mon amie — my (female) friend
  • elle s'appelle — her name is
  • aussi — also / too
  • contents d'être ici — happy to be here
🇫🇷 PART 3 · SE SALUER EN FRANCE 12 min

In France, a greeting is not optional — you say bonjour when you enter a shop, board a bus, or approach a desk, and leaving one out can read as rude. As the day turns to evening (roughly after 6 p.m.), bonjour gives way to bonsoir. Among friends and family, people often greet with la bise — a light kiss on each cheek — though the number of kisses varies by region.

Above all, remember the choice from Day 3: tu or vous. Use vous with someone you’ve just met, an adult, or anyone you wish to show respect; save tu for friends and peers. That single choice is what makes your first impression land as polite rather than presumptuous — register matters from the very first word.

⚜ PART 4 · UNE VERTU FRANÇAISE 5 min
LE COURAGE · COURAGE

Jeanne d’Arc (c. 1412–1431) was a teenage peasant girl who, in 1429, rallied the French forces at the siege of Orléans against the English — turning the tide of the Hundred Years’ War. She had no rank, no training, and every reason to stay silent; instead she stepped forward and spoke.

Courage often begins with the smallest, hardest first step — being the one to speak up when it would be easier to say nothing. Making a good first impression in a new language takes a version of that same courage: the willingness to open your mouth and try. « Je ne crains rien, car Dieu est avec moi. »
