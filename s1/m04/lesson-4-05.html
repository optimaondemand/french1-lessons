Lesson 4.05
⚜ L’emploi du temps de Julie — Reading & Culture
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
See every word from this week living inside one real French schedule — and hear it read aloud.
Julie is a collégienne. Her school day uses your times, your school words, and the 24-hour clock — all at once. Read and listen, then step back to see how France keeps time, and meet a writer whose honesty took real courage. Keep the module's question in mind: how does the way a culture keeps time reveal what it values?
📖 PART 1 · LA LECTURE 15 min

Read each paragraph, then press ▶ Écouter and follow along with your eyes. Read it a second time with the audio playing — that pairing of eye and ear is where fluency starts.

Le matin, mon premier cours commence à huit heures. J’ai maths, puis français.

À midi, nous avons une pause déjeuner d’une heure. L’après-midi, j’ai histoire et sport.

Les cours finissent à dix-sept heures. Le mercredi, il n’y a pas cours l’après-midi !

🖌️ PART 2 · ANNOTE LE TEXTE 8 min

On a printout or in your notes, circle every time expression (à huit heures, à midi, à dix-sept heures) and underline the school words (cours, pause déjeuner). Notice one time is written the French way — dix-sept heures — and ask yourself what time that is on a 12-hour clock.

A few words you haven't formally studied, glossed here so the passage reads smoothly:
  • premier — first
  • puis — then
  • d’une heure — an hour long
  • finissent — (they) finish
  • il n’y a pas cours — there's no class
🇫🇷 PART 3 · COMMENT LA FRANCE GARDE LE TEMPS 12 min

Notice how Julie ends her day: les cours finissent à dix-sept heures — 17h, not “five o'clock.” Across France — schools, train stations, TV listings, invitations — time is told on the 24-hour clock. A movie starts à 20h30; a train leaves à 14h05. Learning to read it is part of reading a French day correctly.

The French school day is long — often 8h to 17h — but broken by a real sit-down lunch and, traditionally, a free Wednesday afternoon: Julie's il n'y a pas cours l'après-midi on Wednesdays is normal, a slot long reserved for sport, music, and family. Punctuality for scheduled events is expected, and a full, structured day is seen as a serious one.

Back to our question: a culture that puts a precise 24-hour time on everything — yet guards a free Wednesday and a slow lunch — is telling you what it values: order and rest, both taken seriously.

⚜ PART 4 · UNE VERTU FRANÇAISE 5 min
L'HONNÊTETÉ · HONESTY

In 1898 the writer Émile Zola (1840–1902) published an open letter on the front page of a Paris newspaper under the headline « J'accuse ! ». He accused the French army of knowingly convicting an innocent officer, Captain Alfred Dreyfus, and hiding the truth. Zola knew it could cost him everything — he was tried, convicted of libel, and fled the country — but the letter helped turn public opinion and, eventually, clear Dreyfus.

Honesty isn't only telling the truth to others — it's being truthful about ourselves, including how we actually spend our hours. As you build your own emploi du temps this module, be honest about where your time really goes. « La vérité est en marche, et rien ne l'arrêtera. »
