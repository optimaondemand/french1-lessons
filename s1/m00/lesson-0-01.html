⚜ L'alphabet français — The French Alphabet
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Welcome to French. Before any words, you need the sounds — and it starts with 26 familiar letters that suddenly have new names.
The French alphabet uses the same 26 letters as English, but almost every one is named differently. This whole module is about your ear and your mouth, not memorizing words — so today, just listen and repeat. By the end of two weeks you'll read French aloud and sound like you mean it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and say the 26 French letter names.
2
I will be able to spell my own name aloud in French.
⏰ PART 1 · BIENVENUE 8 min

French is spoken on five continents by roughly 300 million people — and for the next year, by you. This module is your on-ramp: sounds, letters, accents, and numbers, so that when real words arrive next module, you can already say them.

Watch these five — they trip up every English speaker:

  • E is a soft “euh”, nothing like English “ee.”
  • G (“jé”) and J (“ji”) are swapped from what English ears expect.
  • H (“ache”) is never pronounced at the start of a word.
  • W is literally “double vé” — a double-V, not a double-U.
  • Y is “i grec” — “the Greek i.”
✍️ PART 3 · ÉPELLE TON NOM 10 min

Spelling aloud — épeler — is one of the most useful real-world skills in a new language (giving your name, an email, a street). Using the buttons above, spell your first and last name out loud, letter by letter, in French. Do it three times until it flows.

Then try a friend's name, and the word FRANÇAIS (you'll need the ç — that's tomorrow’s lesson).

⭐ POUR ALLER PLUS LOIN · optional · +7 min
Go back through the grid and shadow each letter: play it, pause, and repeat it three times, matching the voice exactly. Pay special attention to the vowels A-E-I-O-U — they carry French pronunciation.
