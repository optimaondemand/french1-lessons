Lesson 0.04
⚜ Les sons II — Nasal Vowels & Liaison
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Two sounds carry the whole French “accent” — the nasal vowels, and the way words link together.
If yesterday was about what French drops, today is about what makes it flow. Nasal vowels give French its music; liaison is why les amis sounds like one connected word. Get these two, and you sound French.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and produce the three main nasal vowels (on, an, in).
2
I will be able to hear liaison — a silent final consonant linking to the next word's vowel.
👃 PART 1 · LES VOYELLES NASALES 16 min
What's a nasal vowel? When a vowel is followed by n or m, the air goes partly through your nose and the n/m mostly disappears. You don't say the “n” — the vowel just takes on a humming, nasal color.

Three families cover most of French. Play each and let the sound resonate:

  • bongood — the on sound (no “n” said)
  • grandbig/tall — the an sound (n and d silent)
  • vingttwenty — the in sound (“van”)
  • un paina loaf of bread — two nasals in a row (un + in)

Tip: pinch your nose lightly and say bon. If you feel a buzz, you're nasalizing correctly.

🔗 PART 2 · LA LIAISON 14 min
What's liaison? A final consonant that's normally silent wakes up and links to the next word when that word starts with a vowel. It's why French sounds connected, not choppy. Most often the linking sound is a z.
  • les_z_amisthe friends — “lay-zah-MEE”
  • vous_z_avezyou have — “voo-zah-VAY”
  • nous_z_avonswe have — “noo-zah-VON”
  • Comment_t_allez-vous ?How are you? — the t links (“koh-mahn-tah-lay-VOO”)
✅ PART 3 · VÉRIFIE 10 min
In bon, do you pronounce the n?
Why do you hear a “z” in les amis?
Which word contains the an nasal sound?
⭐ POUR ALLER PLUS LOIN · optional · +7 min
Shadow the four liaison phrases until each sounds like a single connected word, with no gap where the link happens. Then say Comment allez-vous ? five times — you'll use it constantly.
