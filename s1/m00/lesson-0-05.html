⚜ Les nombres 1–30 — Numbers
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Your first words you can actually use today — the numbers, the backbone of prices, ages, times, and dates.
Numbers show up in every conversation you'll ever have in French. Learn 1–30 by ear now and you're set for telling time, giving your age, and counting money later. Then meet Louis Braille, who reinvented reading itself — freely, for everyone.

Two patterns save you memorizing: 17–19 are just “ten + digit” (dix-sept = 10-7). And 21–29 are “vingt + digit,” with a little et only in 21 (vingt et un) — the rest are hyphenated (vingt-deux…).
⚜ PART 2 · UNE VERTU FRANÇAISE 6 min
LE SERVICE · SERVICE

Louis Braille (1809–1852) lost his sight as a child in his father's workshop in a small town near Paris. As a teenager at a school for the blind, he invented a system of raised dots that let blind people read and write by touch — a whole alphabet reimagined. He gave it to the world freely, asking nothing.

You spent this week learning an alphabet with your eyes and ears. Braille built one for the fingertips — and made it a gift. That's service: using what you learn to open a door for someone else.
