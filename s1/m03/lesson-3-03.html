Lesson 3.03
⚜ Avoir — the Full Table, Age & the Idioms
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Two things make avoir feel French — the glide of j'ai, and the surprise that you “have” your age. Then you'll conjugate it all the way through, on your own.
Yesterday you met the six forms. Today: how je becomes j'ai before a vowel, and the French habit of using avoir where English uses to be — for age (j'ai quinze ans), hunger (j'ai faim), and thirst (j'ai soif). Then you'll build the full table — and try it yourself before you see the answer.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use elision (je → j') before ai, and use avoir to state age, hunger, and thirst.
2
I will be able to conjugate avoir through all six persons without help.
🎵 PART 1 · RÈGLE N°1 — L'ÉLISION (J'AI) 10 min

When je meets a vowel

Rule 1. Before a vowel sound, je drops its e and adds an apostrophe: j'. Because avoir begins with a vowel, je + ai always becomes j'ai. French avoids two vowel sounds bumping together.

je ai  ❌  →  j'ai  ✅. Listen — you can hear the words glide together:

  • J'ai un frère et une sœur.I have a brother and a sister.
  • J'ai faim et j'ai soif.I'm hungry and thirsty.
🎂 PART 2 · RÈGLE N°2 — AVOIR LÀ OÙ L'ANGLAIS DIT « BE » 10 min

You “have” your age

Rule 2. For age, hunger, and thirst, French uses avoir, not être. You literally have fifteen years: j'ai quinze ans. Don't forget the word ans (years) — j'ai quinze alone is incomplete.

je suis quinze ans  ❌  →  j'ai quinze ans  ✅. Listen — age and appetite, both with avoir:

  • Il a douze ans.He is twelve (years old).
  • J'ai faim et j'ai soif.I'm hungry and thirsty.
⭐ POUR ALLER PLUS LOIN · optional · +7 min
