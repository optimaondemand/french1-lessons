Lesson 3.05
⚜ Voici ma famille — Reading & Culture
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
See every word from this week living inside one real French family — and hear it read aloud.
Hortense introduces her family. Her few short sentences use your vocabulary, your verb avoir, and your ages — all at once. Read and listen, then step back to see how French families work, and meet a leader whose sense of responsibility carried a nation.
📖 PART 1 · LA LECTURE 15 min

Read each paragraph, then press ▶ Écouter and follow along with your eyes. Read it a second time with the audio playing — that pairing of eye and ear is where fluency starts.

Voici ma famille. Nous sommes cinq : mon père, ma mère, mon frère, ma sœur et moi.

Mon frère a dix-huit ans et ma sœur a huit ans. Nous avons aussi un chien.

Mes grands-parents habitent à Nice. J'ai deux cousins et une cousine. J'aime beaucoup ma famille.

🖌️ PART 2 · ANNOTE LE TEXTE 8 min

On a printout or in your notes, circle every form of avoir and underline each family word. You should find three uses of avoir (a, a, avons… and j'ai). Notice how Hortense states ages: a dix-huit ans — have, not be.

A few words you haven't formally studied, glossed here so the passage reads smoothly:
  • voici — here is / this is
  • nous sommes cinq — there are five of us
  • et moi — and me
  • aussi — also / too
  • habitent — (they) live
  • à Nice — in Nice (a city on the Riviera)
  • beaucoup — a lot / very much
🇫🇷 PART 3 · LA FAMILLE FRANÇAISE 12 min

Notice how Hortense says ages: mon frère a dix-huit ans — literally “my brother has eighteen years.” Where English says I am fifteen, French says j'ai quinze ans. It's one of the first habits that marks a real French speaker, so it's worth over-practicing: you don't be your age in French, you have it.

Watch the little possessive words, too: mon père, ma mère, mes grands-parents. French picks the word to match the noun — mon before a masculine word, ma before a feminine one, mes before a plural. You'll study these fully soon; for now, just notice the pattern in the passage. And in France, family often reaches wide — grandparents, aunts, uncles, and cousins are counted as close, and Sunday meals (le déjeuner du dimanche) frequently gather three generations at one table.

⚜ PART 4 · UNE VERTU FRANÇAISE 5 min
LA RESPONSABILITÉ · RESPONSIBILITY

On 18 June 1940, with France fallen and most of its leaders silent, Charles de Gaulle spoke from London over BBC radio — l'Appel du 18 juin. Almost alone, he took responsibility for a nation that had given up on itself, and called the French to keep resisting. Few heard the broadcast that night, but it became the seed of Free France.

Responsibility means answering for the people who depend on you — whether that's a whole country or a family at one table. It's the heart of this module's question: what do we owe the people closest to us? « Quoi qu'il arrive, la flamme de la résistance française ne doit pas s'éteindre et ne s'éteindra pas. »
