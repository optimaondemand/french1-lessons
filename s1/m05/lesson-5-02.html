Lesson 5.02
⚜ Le soir & la règle n°1 — Evening Verbs + Reflexive Pronouns
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you heard the morning. Today you finish the day — and learn why these verbs carry a little word in front of them.
You'll pick up the evening and sequencing words, then meet the idea behind every verb in this module: the reflexive pronoun. The action comes back to the person doing it — I wash myself. Rule 1 is simply matching the right little pronoun to the subject.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and pronounce the evening and sequencing words (words 11–20).
2
I will be able to explain what makes a verb reflexive and match the reflexive pronoun to each subject.

🧠 PART 2 · LA GRAMMAIRE — QU'EST-CE QU'UN VERBE PRONOMINAL ? 17 min

Why every verb has a "se"

A reflexive verb describes an action you do to yourself. In English we usually leave that out — we say "I wash," not "I wash myself." French keeps it. That little word is the reflexive pronoun, and it has to match the subject. That's Rule 1.

SubjectReflexive pronounExample
jemeje me lève
tutetu te lèves
il / elle / onseil se lève
nousnousnous nous levons
vousvousvous vous levez
ils / ellesseils se lèvent

Hear the three you'll use most. Notice the pronoun changes even though the action doesn't:

  • Je me lève.I get up.
  • Tu te lèves.You get up.
  • Il se lève.He gets up.
