Lesson 5.05
⚜ Un matin avec Léa — Reading & Culture
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
See every verb from this week living inside one real French day — and hear it read aloud.
Léa is a lycéenne in Lyon. Her morning uses your vocabulary, your rules, and your sequencing words — all at once. Read and listen, then step back to see how a French day is shaped, and meet a woman whose perseverance changed science.
📖 PART 1 · LA LECTURE 15 min

Read each paragraph, then press ▶ Écouter and follow along with your eyes. Read it a second time with the audio playing — that pairing of eye and ear is where fluency starts.

Je me réveille toujours à six heures et demie, pas parce que je veux, mais parce que mon cours commence à huit heures. D’abord, je me douche pendant dix minutes. Ensuite, je me coiffe et je m’habille. Je me dépêche beaucoup le mardi parce que les bus sont pleins.

Mon père se réveille plus tard que moi, il travaille à la maison. Ma mère, elle, se lève très tôt et se brosse les dents avant tout le monde. Nous nous retrouvons à table pour le petit-déjeuner pendant quinze minutes seulement.

Le soir, je rentre à dix-sept heures. Je me repose un peu, puis je fais mes devoirs. Je me couche normalement à vingt-deux heures trente. Je m’endors vite, je suis toujours fatiguée !

🖌️ PART 2 · ANNOTE LE TEXTE 8 min

On a printout or in your notes, circle every reflexive verb and underline its pronoun. You should find nine or more. Notice the one that isn't reflexive — je fais mes devoirs — and ask yourself why.

A few words you haven't formally studied, glossed here so the passage reads smoothly:
  • pendant — for / during
  • pleins — full (crowded)
  • plus tard que — later than
  • avant tout le monde — before everyone
  • seulement — only
  • puis — then
  • fatiguée — tired
🇫🇷 PART 3 · LA JOURNÉE FRANÇAISE 12 min

A French school day runs long — often 8h to 17h — but it's broken by a real sit-down lunch, sometimes 90 minutes, frequently a hot meal in the cantine. That's why Léa's family gives breakfast only fifteen quick minutes: the day's important meal comes later.

Notice the 24-hour clock: Léa rentre à dix-sept heures (17:00), not "five o'clock." Across France — train stations, school schedules, TV listings, invitations — time is told this way. Getting comfortable with it is part of reading a French day correctly.

⚜ PART 4 · UNE VERTU FRANÇAISE 5 min
LA PERSÉVÉRANCE · PERSEVERANCE

Marie Curie (1867–1934) kept a routine as demanding as any — years of grinding tons of ore by hand in a cold shed to isolate radium. Turned away from universities, working through poverty and discrimination, she persevered and became the first person ever to win two Nobel Prizes, in two different sciences.

A routine isn't glamorous — it's the same small acts, repeated. Curie's life is the proof that repeated small acts, held to over time, are exactly what changes the world. « Rien dans la vie n'est à craindre, tout est à comprendre. »

⭐ POUR ALLER PLUS LOIN · optional · +7 min · comprehension check
Answer from the passage. Instant feedback.
À quelle heure Léa se réveille-t-elle ?
Qui se lève le plus tôt dans la famille ?
Que fait Léa juste avant de se coucher ?
