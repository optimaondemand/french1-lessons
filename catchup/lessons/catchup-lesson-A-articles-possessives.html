Catch-up Lesson A
⚜ Un, une, des & mon, ma, mes — Articles + Possessives
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Every French noun carries a gender — and two families of little words follow that gender everywhere.
You already say le livre and la classe. Today you learn the partners of le and la — un, une, des (a, an, some) — and the words for my, your, his, her, our, their. Both change with the noun, so you learn them once and use them in every sentence about your family from now on.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to choose le / la / l' / les or un / une / des to match a noun's gender and number.
2
I will be able to choose the possessive (mon, ma, mes…) that matches the thing I'm talking about.
⏰ PART 1 · SE METTRE EN ROUTE 3 min
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
In English, does the word my change when you talk about your brother instead of your sister?
📚 PART 2 · LES ARTICLES — THE, A, SOME 12 min

Two sets of little words, one rule

You've learned every noun with le, la, l' or les in front — that's the word for the. Each one has a partner that means a / an (and, in the plural, some). Both sets follow the noun's gender and number.

MasculineFemininePlural (m. or f.)
thele livrela classeles livres
a / an / someun livreune classedes livres

Three things to notice:

  • Before a vowel, le and la shrink to l' (l'élève) — but un and une never shrink: un élève (a boy student), une élève (a girl student).
  • Plural nouns add -s, and you can't hear it: livre and livres sound the same. The article tells your ear it's plural — le sounds like “luh,” les like “lay.”
  • French never drops the article. English says “I'm looking at books”; French says Je regarde des livres.

Play each one and say it back out loud — listen for the little word in front:

  • le livrethe book
  • un livrea book
  • des livres(some) books
  • la classethe class
  • une classea class
  • Je regarde un livre.I'm looking at a book.
🧠 PART 3 · LES POSSESSIFS — MY, YOUR, HIS, HER… 14 min

Match the thing, not the owner

The rule: a possessive matches the noun that comes after it — masculine, feminine, or plural — exactly like le / la / les. Read across each row.

Owner+ masculine noun+ feminine noun+ plural noun
je → mymon pèrema mèremes parents
tu → yourton frèreta sœurtes grands-parents
il / elle → his / herson filssa filleses parents
nous → ournotre pèrenotre mèrenos parents
vous → yourvotre frèrevotre sœurvos parents
ils / elles → theirleur filsleur filleleurs parents
Two surprises. (1) son père means his father or her father — French matches père (masculine), not the owner. (2) Before a vowel, ma / ta / sa become mon / ton / son so the words glide: mon école, mon amie.

Hear them in sentences, and say each one back out loud:

  • Mon père est professeur.My father is a teacher.
  • Ma sœur est étudiante.My sister is a student.
  • Mes grands-parents sont français.My grandparents are French.
  • Léa regarde son père.Léa is looking at her father.
✅ PART 4 · VÉRIFIE TA COMPRÉHENSION 8 min

Pick the word that fits. Take your time — look at the noun first, then choose. Instant feedback; this is practice, not a grade.

✎ ITEM 1

✎ ITEM 2

✎ ITEM 3

✎ ITEM 4

✎ ITEM 5
J'aime ______ école. (my — école starts with a vowel)
✎ ITEM 6
Léa regarde ______ père. (her)
